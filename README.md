Although the Problem Gambling Severity Index (PGSI) is one of the most commonly employed measures of problem gambling severity, Currie et al. (2013) proposed revised thresholds (R-PGSI) to improve the differentiation between low- and moderate-risk gamblers. However, few studies have evaluated whether one scoring method offers greater utility or more accurately distinguishes gamblers across levels of severity. Given this, the present exploratory study compared the standard (S-PGSI) and R-PGSI scoring thresholds. Specifically, analyses examined whether individuals reclassified downward under the R-PGSI more closely resembled S-PGSI low-risk or R-PGSI moderate-risk groups using multidimensional measures of impulsivity and erroneous gambling cognitions. A total of (n = 630) gamblers aged between 18 and 73 (Mage = 38.56, SDage = 11.52) completed an online survey consisting of the Gambling-Related Cognitions Scale (GRCS), Short UPPS-P Impulsive Behavioural Scale (S-UPPS-P) and PGSI. Overall classification agreement across PGSI scoring methods was high (86.3%; κ = .81, κw = .94), although 13.7% (n = 86) were reclassified downward from S-PGSI moderate-risk to R-PGSI low-risk. Games–Howell post-hoc comparisons indicated that reclassified individuals occupied a distinct intermediate position on negative urgency and perceived inability to stop gambling, aligned with moderate-risk individuals on positive urgency, and resembled low-risk individuals on interpretive bias. Overall, the evidence from the current study does not suggest that one scoring method is superior; rather, both appear to be valid. Findings support conceptualising gambling along a spectrum and developing interventions tailored to individuals' gambling severity and the psychological risk factors that underpin it.
